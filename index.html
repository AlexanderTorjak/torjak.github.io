DWG 01

E5-P0 — Obstacle Avoidance Robot

June 2025 – April 2026
MechanicalEmbeddedControls

An 8″ x 9″ x 4″ autonomous car, built to test my ability to design a chassis around an electrical system and hold up an interdisciplinary project end to end.

  • 3D modeled and printed a chassis that mounted four motors using hardware-store screws — no custom fasteners required.
  • Researched and tested batteries to find one that could supply the needed current for the target runtime and still recharge reliably.
  • Selected and configured three ultrasonic sensors to detect obstacles and measure distance to collision.
  • Programmed an Arduino Mega with two PID control loops — one for forward/backward speed, one for the left–right motor differential that sets the turn radius.

DWG 03

NASA 16-Psyche Articulating Wheel Set

August 2023 – May 2024
MechanicalDynamics

A wheel-and-suspension system prototyped for a rover intended to traverse the surface of asteroid 16 Psyche.

  • Applied dynamics, rigid-body physics, and statics to predict rover velocity and acceleration across four surfaces with different friction coefficients.
  • Estimated the inclines and slope angles the rover would struggle to climb, and used that to set design limits on dimensions and center of mass.
  • Ran experimental trials over simulated terrain — concrete, sand, and a rock–sand mix — timing travel over a fixed distance at varying angles.
  • Compared experimental results against simulation to validate design decisions, then presented findings to both domain experts and a general audience.
DWG 04

Magnetic Levitator

January – April 2024
ControlsAnalog Circuit

A PD feedback controller that levitates a metallic object at a fixed distance beneath an electromagnet.

  • Specified coil windings and wire diameter for the levitated mass, then designed an analog PID circuit around op-amps and MOSFETs.
  • Chose potentiometers to stabilize the controller's gain values.
  • Found that potentiometer resolution wasn't precise enough to hold the object stable past three seconds — a hardware limit that clarified why software-based control matters.
DWG 05

Sensor-Automated Christmas Lights

November 2023
EmbeddedSensors

A final project for a sensors-and-actuators course: use three different sensor types to drive one output through an Arduino.

  • Built a circuit combining a photoresistor, a microphone, and a capacitive touch switch to interpret multiple sensor inputs.
  • Used an Arduino Uno to process that data and switch an AC-sourced relay controlling a strand of LED lights.
  • Learned how digital logic and multi-sensor fusion apply to real systems, and how to read a datasheet to choose and calibrate the right sensor for the job.
